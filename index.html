Definitions, assumptions and limitations
Sample data only
Every number on this page is synthetic. National totals are set near the scale of a high-burden country (about 18 million cases and 43,000 deaths a year) but are not WHO figures. There are 9 fictional states and 43 districts; the EMR exchange is live in 5 of the 9 states.
WHO estimate
Modelled total burden, including cases and deaths that never reach a health service.
Expected per system
The estimate multiplied by the share expected to pass through that system. In practice the shares would come from care-seeking surveys (DHS/MIS), facility listings and system coverage. Deaths use different shares from cases because more deaths occur at home.
No formal care
Burden outside any reporting system. It is a modelling gap rather than a reporting gap, so it is excluded from gap rankings.
% captured
Reported divided by expected, for a system and location.
Data quality dimensions
Reporting completeness: units that submitted ÷ units expected. Timeliness: reports on time ÷ reports expected. Data element completeness: non-missing key fields ÷ expected. Internal consistency: reports passing logic and outlier checks. Recording accuracy: reported ÷ (expected × completeness). External consistency: agreement of completeness-adjusted reported with an independent survey-based benchmark, folded so over- and under-reporting both lower it. The data quality score on the key figures is the simple average of the available dimensions; dimensions with no reporting units are skipped.
Minimum detectable change (MDC)
Compares the latest n months with the previous n months at 5% significance and 80% power: MDC = (1.96 + 0.84) × √(2 × φ × μ ÷ n), shown as a percentage of the baseline monthly level μ. The series is standardised to average completeness and divided by a monthly seasonal index. φ is the variance ÷ mean of the last 24 months around a linear trend (minimum 1).
Reporting-artefact noise
The spread of monthly reporting completeness around its trend, divided by its mean. It is the apparent change in counts caused only by fluctuating reporting.
Effective MDC
√(MDC² + artefact noise²). Anything above the chosen target (10% by default) is flagged as unable to detect that size of change. Pooling channels usually lowers it because their noise is partly independent.
Detection power
Probability of detecting a change, using the effective MDC as 2.8 standard errors. It is an approximation: normal distribution, overdispersed counts, seasonality removed, otherwise stable reporting.
